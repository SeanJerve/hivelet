                                 1 INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background of the Study

         Administrative processes in apartment management typically involve
tenant record keeping, financial tracking, and maintenance coordination, which
become increasingly complex as the number of units increases (Burke, 2026).
While web-based property management systems have been developed to
support these functions, their adoption in small-scale residential settings remains
limited due to operational constraints such as limited infrastructure, technical
capacity, and resource availability (Mia et al., 2024).

         In small-scale boarding house operations, administrative tasks are still
managed using standalone spreadsheet files and manual documentation. In
observed cases within the study context, these records are stored in removable
storage devices, exposing the system to risks in data integrity such as accidental
deletion, file corruption, duplication, and lack of version control. In addition,
tenant concerns and maintenance requests are often communicated through
informal channels such as messaging applications or verbal reporting, resulting
in untracked requests, delayed responses, and inconsistent follow-up.

         These conditions reflect fragmented administrative workflows, where
records, communication, and transactions are managed across separate,
non-integrated tools. Consequently, property owners may experience difficulties
in maintaining accurate records, monitoring financial transactions, and ensuring
                                                                                                           2
         timely resolution of tenant concerns. Although existing web-based
property management systems aim to address such inefficiencies through
centralized data storage and real-time processing (Kotkar et al., 2026), many are
commonly designed with assumptions aligned to large-scale or commercial
property environments. These systems typically assume stable infrastructure,
structured workflows, and dedicated administrative personnel (Ullah et al., 2021),
which do not reflect the operational conditions of small-scale,
resource-constrained boarding houses.

         In the Philippine context, these challenges are further reinforced by
reliance on cash-based transactions, limited access to integrated digital tools,
and continued dependence on manual record-keeping practices (Mia et al.,
2024). As a result, many small-scale apartment operations continue to function
under fragmented and informal administrative processes despite the availability
of existing digital solutions.

         These limitations are also reflected in local implementations. Magno et al.
(2024) developed an apartment rental management system to address issues
such as disorganized data management, difficulty in tracking tenant payments,
missed billing deadlines, and reliance on paper-based records. Their findings
demonstrate that digitizing transaction tracking and task organization improves
administrative efficiency and data accuracy. However, the system primarily
focuses on financial transactions and task management, without integrating
communication, booking, and tenant interaction workflows into a unified system.
                                                                                                           3
         Similarly, Pavia et al. (2022) developed a property management system
for Hospitality Management students at Bicol College, demonstrating how digital
platforms can enhance coordination, record-keeping, and service management
within a controlled academic environment with predefined workflows and
institutional support. While the study confirms the effectiveness of system-based
management, these conditions differ significantly from small-scale boarding
house operations, where administrative processes are informal, decentralized,
and constrained by limited resources.

         Taken together, these local studies demonstrate that while digital solutions
can improve specific aspects of property management, they are often
implemented either as function-specific systems or within controlled
environments. As such, they do not fully address the fragmented and
non-integrated nature of administrative workflows in real-world,
resource-constrained boarding house settings.

         Many existing systems therefore remain misaligned with the operational
realities of small-scale, apartment management environments. This reveals a
critical gap: the absence of an integrated platform that consolidates tenant
management, financial tracking, communication, and booking within a single,
context-appropriate system.

         This study proposes Hivelet, a web-based apartment management system
designed to address these limitations by centralizing administrative processes
and providing a structured mechanism for managing tenant records, financial
                                                                                                           4
transactions, and maintenance concerns within a unified platform, specifically
tailored for resource-constrained residential settings.

1.2 Objectives of the Study

         The primary objective of this study is to address the mismatch between
existing property management systems and the operational realities of
small-scale, resource-constrained boarding houses by designing, developing,
and evaluating Hivelet, a web-based apartment management system that
improves administrative workflow integration, communication, and operational
control. Specifically, the study aims:

    1. To analyze the existing apartment management practices of the Fe
         Galang Da Silva Apartment to determine the challenges in tenant
         management, financial tracking, communication, and booking processes,
         and establish system requirements based on the identified operational
         gaps and user needs.

    2. To develop the Hivelet system to feature tenant and room management,
         booking and reservation management, financial tracking with optional
         online payment recording, a maintenance ticketing and notification
         system, role-based access control for administrators and tenants, and
         Progressive Web Application (PWA) support for responsive cross-device
         accessibility.
                                                                                                           5
    3. To pilot test the developed Hivelet system for functionality,

         responsiveness, and operational performance.
    4. To evaluate and optimize the system based on the ISO/IEC 25010

         software quality model in terms of: Functional suitability, Performance
         efficiency, Compatibility, Usability, Reliability, Security, Maintainability,
         Portability

1.3 Significance of the Study

         The development of Hivelet, a web-based apartment management system
implemented as a Progressive Web Application (PWA), is significant in
addressing the operational challenges of fragmented and manual administrative
workflows in small-scale, resource-constrained boarding house environments.

         For property administrators and owners, the study provides a
centralized system that reduces dependence on manual record-keeping
practices such as spreadsheets and removable storage devices, which are prone
to data inconsistency, loss, and duplication. By integrating tenant records,
financial transactions, and maintenance concerns into a unified platform, the
system supports more reliable information management and improves
administrative coordination in environments affected by cash-based transactions
and limited digital infrastructure.
                                                                                                           6
         For tenants, the study improves communication efficiency by providing a
structured mechanism for submitting and tracking maintenance concerns and
viewing relevant account information. This reduces reliance on informal
communication channels such as messaging applications and verbal reporting,
which often result in delayed responses and untracked requests.

         For future researchers, the study contributes to a localized
implementation of a Progressive Web Application­based property management
system designed specifically for small-scale residential contexts. The system
architecture, integration approach, and evaluation using the ISO/IEC 25010
model may serve as a reference for further research, particularly in developing
systems that address the gap between enterprise-level property management
solutions and resource-constrained residential operations.

1.4 Scope and Delimitations

         This study focuses on the design, development, and evaluation of Hivelet,
a Progressive Web Application (PWA) created to transition the 32-unit Fe Galang
Da Silva Apartment near Bicol University from manual, paper-based
administration to a centralized digital management system. To achieve this,
Hivelet integrates comprehensive room and tenant management capabilities,
facilitating seamless onboarding, the secure digital storage of records, real-time
monitoring of bed availability, and a public-facing booking module that allows
                                                                                                           7
prospective renters to submit reservation inquiries without requiring full system
access. Furthermore, the application features a financial tracking component
dedicated to monitoring cash flow, expenses, and tenant payments; while this
primarily supports manual, cash-based transaction recording, it offers an optional
digital payment submission feature for added convenience. Operational
communication is also streamlined through a structured issue ticketing system,
enabling tenants to log maintenance requests that administrators can
systematically assign, track, and update until resolution.

         Despite these comprehensive features, the scope is bound by specific
delimitations regarding system accessibility and financial processing: although
the PWA utilizes cached resources for limited offline access and responsive
design for diverse devices, core functionalities--such as data encoding, real-time
synchronization, and booking updates--strictly require an active internet
connection to maintain data consistency. Additionally, the supplementary online
payment feature is restricted to basic transaction recording and explicitly
excludes advanced financial processing capabilities like automated
reconciliation, refunds, or direct banking integration. Ultimately, to ensure the
system effectively resolves the apartment's operational inefficiencies, Hivelet will
be rigorously evaluated against the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standard,
with a specific emphasis on measuring its functional suitability, performance
efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and
portability
                        2 THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

         This chapter establishes the theoretical and empirical foundation of the
Hivelet system by synthesizing literature on property management systems,
Management Information Systems (MIS), financial analytics, digital
communication systems, and Progressive Web Applications (PWA). Rather than
treating these domains independently, the study positions them as
interconnected operational components within apartment management
environments. The framework argues that inefficiencies in small-scale residential
operations emerge primarily from fragmented administrative structures, where
records, financial monitoring, communication, and operational coordination exist
across disconnected tools and processes. The reviewed literature collectively
supports the need for an integrated, centralized, and accessible management
system tailored for resource-constrained apartment environments.

2.1 Review of Related Literature

2.1.1 Fragmentation and Operational Inefficiencies in Property Management

         The increasing complexity of apartment and boarding house operations
has intensified the need for structured and centralized management systems.
Existing literature consistently identifies fragmentation of administrative
workflows as one of the primary operational issues in residential property
management environments (Ullah et al., 2021; Burke, 2026; Setty, 2022). As the
number of tenants, transactions, and maintenance activities increases, traditional
methods such as manual documentation, spreadsheet-based tracking, and
                                                                                                         11
         informal communication becomes increasingly difficult to manage
consistently and accurately.

         Ullah et al. (2021) explain that conventional property management
practices often suffer from disconnected workflows, documentation
inconsistencies, and inefficient coordination between operational tasks. Similarly,
Burke (2026) emphasizes that increasing residential density proportionally
increases administrative complexity, particularly in areas involving tenant
monitoring, maintenance coordination, and financial recording. These findings
collectively suggest that operational inefficiencies are not isolated technical
issues but structural problems caused by fragmented information handling and
disconnected administrative processes.

         This condition is further reflected in localized implementations. Magno et
al. (2024) observed that apartment administrators commonly experience
difficulties in tracking payments, organizing tenant records, and managing
paper-based transactions. Their findings demonstrate that digitization improves
data consistency and transaction monitoring; however, the study also reveals that
partial digital implementation alone does not fully eliminate fragmentation when
operational functions remain separated. Likewise, Pavia et al. (2022)
demonstrate that system-based management improves organizational
coordination and service operations, yet their implementation operates within a
controlled institutional environment with predefined workflows and technical
support, conditions that differ significantly from resource-constrained apartment
operations.
                                                                                                         12
         Collectively, these studies establish that the primary challenge in
apartment management is not merely the absence of digital systems, but the
absence of integrated operational structures capable of consolidating
administrative functions into a unified environment. This reinforces the need for
centralized information management approaches capable of organizing
fragmented workflows into cohesive and traceable system processes.

2.1.2 Management Information Systems as the Foundation of Centralization

         This theoretical foundation is grounded in Management Information
Systems (MIS), which emphasize the transformation of fragmented operational
data into centralized and actionable information for decision-making. According
to Laudon and Laudon, MIS functions as an organized system that collects,
processes, stores, and distributes information to support operational control,
coordination, and administrative decision-making. Within apartment management
environments, this framework explains why manual and semi-digital operations
often become inefficient, as information is typically distributed across isolated
records, disconnected tools, and inconsistent communication channels.

         This relationship is further illustrated in Figure 1, based on the
Management Information System (MIS) framework by Laudon and Laudon,
which demonstrates the flow of data from fragmented operational sources
through system processing mechanisms, resulting in consolidated and
meaningful information outputs that support organizational decision-making.
                                                                                                         13

  Figure 1. Management Information System (MIS) framework by Laudon and Laudon
         The importance of centralized information processing is further reinforced

by Ghasemaghaei et al. (2018), who emphasize that organizational
decision-making performance significantly improves when operational data is
structured, integrated, and analytically accessible. Their findings indicate that
data analytics competency enables more accurate forecasting, operational
monitoring, and financial oversight, particularly in environments requiring
continuous administrative coordination. Similarly, Pressman and Maxim (2020)
explain that integrated software systems improve consistency, maintainability,
and operational reliability by reducing redundancy and streamlining data flow
across system components.
                                                                                                         14
         Within apartment management operations, these principles directly
support the need for centralized architectures capable of integrating tenant
information, maintenance records, communication logs, and financial
transactions within a single operational environment. The literature therefore
positions MIS not simply as a technological concept, but as the structural
mechanism through which fragmented administrative activities become
coordinated, traceable, and manageable.

         This theoretical perspective also aligns with findings from property
management studies which indicate that operational inefficiencies often emerge
when information systems lack interoperability and centralized accessibility
(Burke, 2026; Magno et al., 2024). Consequently, the integration of operational
data within a unified system environment becomes essential for improving
administrative visibility, reducing redundancy, and supporting informed
decision-making processes.

2.1.3 Financial Analytics and Administrative Decision Support

         Financial management serves as one of the most critical operational
components within apartment administration, as payment monitoring, expense
tracking, and cash flow visibility directly influence managerial decision-making.
Existing studies consistently emphasize that financial inefficiencies often emerge
when payment records are maintained through fragmented or manual processes
(Encarnacion et al., 2025; Magno et al., 2024; Setty, 2022).
                                                                                                         15
         Encarnacion et al. (2025) demonstrate that structured digital payment
monitoring systems significantly improve administrative oversight within student
boarding house operations by providing organized financial ledgers, payment
summaries, and transaction visibility. Their findings suggest that centralized
financial records reduce monitoring inconsistencies and improve forecasting
accuracy. Similarly, Magno et al. (2024) observed that digital transaction tracking
improves billing management and minimizes missed payment monitoring
compared to paper-based systems. Setty (2022) further explains that centralized
financial management systems improve transparency and reduce administrative
errors by consolidating transaction records into a unified database environment.

         These findings strongly align with MIS principles regarding centralized
information processing and structured data accessibility. Ghasemaghaei et al.
(2018) argue that analytical decision-making depends heavily on the quality,
consistency, and accessibility of operational data. In apartment management
contexts, financial analytics therefore functions not merely as a reporting
mechanism, but as a decision-support component that enables administrators to
monitor operational performance, identify irregularities, and maintain financial
accountability.

         Collectively, the literature suggests that financial monitoring becomes
significantly more effective when integrated into a centralized operational
framework rather than implemented as a standalone feature. This reinforces the
necessity of integrating financial analytics within broader apartment management
                                                                                                         16
systems to support consistent administrative control and operational
transparency.

2.1.4 Digital Communication and Structured Issue Resolution

         Beyond financial management, operational efficiency in apartment
environments is also heavily influenced by communication quality and
maintenance coordination processes. Existing literature identifies informal
communication practices as major contributors to delayed responses,
inconsistent issue tracking, and reduced tenant satisfaction (Jing & Lim, 2021;
Saputra et al., 2025).

         Jing and Lim (2021) explain that management service quality significantly
affects residential satisfaction and operational effectiveness within apartment
environments. Their study highlights that responsiveness, communication
consistency, and issue resolution efficiency are critical components of effective
property administration. However, in traditional management settings, tenant
concerns are often communicated through verbal reporting or messaging
applications, resulting in undocumented requests and inconsistent follow-up
procedures.

         To address these issues, Saputra et al. (2025) demonstrate that
web-based room management systems with structured issue coordination
mechanisms significantly improve operational accountability and response
management. By transforming tenant complaints into recorded and traceable
                                                                                                         17
maintenance workflows, digital ticketing systems improve monitoring consistency
and reduce communication gaps between administrators and tenants.

         These findings collectively indicate that communication systems should
not be treated merely as supplementary features but as integral operational
components within apartment management environments. Consistent with MIS
theory, structured communication mechanisms improve information traceability,
coordination efficiency, and administrative visibility by ensuring that operational
concerns are documented within centralized system workflows rather than
dispersed across informal channels.

         The literature therefore supports the integration of maintenance ticketing
and structured communication modules within unified apartment management
systems to improve accountability, responsiveness, and operational coordination.

2.1.5 Progressive Web Applications as an Accessibility and Integration
Framework

         While centralized systems improve operational coordination, effective
implementation also depends on accessibility across devices and varying
infrastructure conditions. Progressive Web Applications (PWAs) provide a
technological framework capable of addressing these accessibility challenges
within resource-constrained environments (Google, 2018; Biørn-Hansen et al.,
2019).
                                                                                                         18
         Google (2018) describes PWAs as web applications that combine the
accessibility of browser-based systems with functionalities commonly associated
with native mobile applications, including responsiveness, offline capability, and
cross-device compatibility. Similarly, Biørn-Hansen et al. (2019) explain that
PWAs reduce development complexity by enabling a single application
architecture to operate consistently across multiple platforms without requiring
separate native implementations.

         These characteristics are particularly relevant within small-scale
apartment operations where infrastructure limitations, inconsistent connectivity,
and device diversity may restrict adoption of conventional enterprise systems.
Mia et al. (2024) further emphasize that small and medium-sized enterprises in
the Philippines commonly experience barriers related to infrastructure limitations,
technical capacity, and resource availability during digital technology adoption.
This suggests that accessibility and implementation simplicity are critical
considerations in system deployment within localized operational environments.

         From a systems perspective, PWA architecture functions as the
technological delivery layer that enables centralized MIS functions to remain
accessible and operational across varying conditions. Rather than serving solely
as a development approach, PWAs support the broader objective of operational
integration by ensuring that administrative tools, communication workflows, and
financial monitoring systems remain consistently accessible across devices and
environments.
                                                                                                         19
2.1.6 Synthesis of Literature

         The reviewed literature collectively demonstrates that operational
inefficiencies in apartment management environments primarily emerge from
fragmented administrative structures rather than the complete absence of digital
technologies. Property management studies identify persistent issues involving
disconnected workflows, inconsistent record management, delayed
communication, and operational redundancy (Burke, 2026; Ullah et al., 2021;
Magno et al., 2024). MIS literature explains these inefficiencies as consequences
of fragmented information processing and lack of centralized operational visibility
(Ghasemaghaei et al., 2018; Pressman & Maxim, 2020).

         Financial analytics studies further establish that structured and centralized
financial data significantly improves administrative oversight, forecasting
capability, and operational transparency (Encarnacion et al., 2025; Setty, 2022).
Similarly, communication and maintenance coordination studies emphasize that
operational accountability improves when issue resolution processes are
formalized through structured digital workflows rather than informal
communication channels (Jing & Lim, 2021; Saputra et al., 2025).

         Meanwhile, PWA literature provides the technological framework that
enables these integrated operational components to remain accessible within
environments characterized by limited infrastructure and varying device
conditions (Google, 2018; Biørn-Hansen et al., 2019; Mia et al., 2024).
                                                                                                         20
         Despite their complementarity, these studies are commonly implemented
in isolation. Existing systems frequently focus on specific operational functions
such as financial management, communication, or tenant tracking independently,
resulting in partial solutions that fail to address the interconnected nature of
apartment management workflows. Consequently, the literature reveals a need
for an integrated operational framework that centralizes administrative
processes, formalizes communication, supports financial monitoring, and
remains accessible within resource-constrained residential environments.

2.2 Gap Bridged by the Study

         Based on the synthesis of literature, a significant research gap emerges
regarding the absence of an integrated apartment management system
specifically designed for small-scale and resource-constrained residential
operations. Existing systems are commonly developed either as
enterprise-oriented platforms or as isolated functional solutions focusing on
individual administrative tasks such as payment monitoring, tenant management,
or maintenance coordination (Magno et al., 2024; Setty, 2022; Saputra et al.,
2025).

         While these systems demonstrate improvements in specific operational
areas, they frequently assume stable infrastructure, dedicated administrative
personnel, and structured operational environments that do not reflect the
realities of small-scale apartment and boarding house operations (Mia et al.,
                                                                                                         21
2024; Burke, 2026). As a result, many administrators continue to rely on
fragmented workflows involving manual documentation, spreadsheet-based
records, informal communication channels, and disconnected transaction
monitoring processes.

         Furthermore, although studies support the importance of centralized
information systems, financial analytics, structured communication, and
accessible deployment architectures, few implementations integrate these
components into a single operational framework tailored for localized,
resource-constrained residential settings.

         This study addresses this gap through the development of Hivelet, a
Progressive Web Application-based apartment management system designed
specifically for small-scale apartment operations. The system integrates
centralized MIS principles, financial tracking mechanisms, structured issue
ticketing, tenant management, booking coordination, and accessible cross-device
deployment within a unified operational environment.

         By consolidating these interconnected administrative functions into a
single platform, the study transforms fragmented workflows into cohesive,
traceable, and structured system processes, thereby aligning theoretical
principles with practical implementation within real-world residential management
contexts.

2.3 Concept of the Study

  This study is grounded on the concept of improving apartment

management by integrating its core processes into a centralized digital platform.

Apartment operations involve interconnected activities such as tenant

management, payment tracking, booking coordination, and maintenance

handling. In traditional setups, these processes are often managed manually and

separately, resulting in delays, inconsistencies, and increased administrative

workload.

  Figure 1 illustrates the study's conceptual framework using the

Input-Process-Output (IPO) model, a standard software engineering approach

that maps how system components interact to transform inputs into outputs.

           Figure 2. Conceptual Framework
                                                                                                         23
         In this study, the Input phase consists of the study objectives, review of
related literature, client interviews, needfinding observations, and analysis of
existing administrative and payment workflows. These inputs provide both
theoretical and contextual foundations, allowing the researchers to identify
operational gaps, user requirements, and system constraints within the
apartment management environment. The Process phase applies the Agile
Software Development Life Cycle, integrating iterative stages of planning, design,
development, testing, deployment, and review. Within this phase, the system
modules are implemented, including financial tracking (analytics and reports),
booking management (reservation logic), tenant management (profiles and
records), and an optional online payment integration using the Adyen API. These
processes transform identified requirements into a functional and structured
system aligned with real-world operational needs. The Output phase includes the
developed Hivelet system, its functional modules, and generated outputs such as
financial reports, booking records, and transaction receipts. It also includes the
completed research manuscript, system evaluation results based on ISO/IEC
25010 standards, and the user manual. These outputs reflect the system's
effectiveness in improving administrative efficiency, data organization, and
communication within the apartment management process. Feedback is
incorporated across all phases, allowing continuous system refinement based on
user evaluation and operational performance, ensuring that the system remains
adaptive and responsive to real-world conditions.

designed to centralize and integrate tenant management, financial tracking,
booking inquiries, and maintenance issue reporting into a unified operational
platform that supports small-scale, resource-constrained administrative
environments.

         Progressive Web Application (PWA) ­ A web-based application
architecture that enables app-like functionality through browsers, providing
responsive design, offline capability, and cross-device accessibility without
requiring installation, making it suitable for low-resource and hybrid operational
environments.

         Resource-Constrained Environment ­ Refers to small-scale residential
or boarding house operations characterized by limited financial resources,
minimal technical infrastructure, and constrained administrative manpower,
where management processes are typically dependent on hybrid manual and
basic digital tools rather than fully integrated systems.

         Fragmented Workflow ­ A condition in which administrative processes
such as tenant management, financial recording, and maintenance reporting are
handled using disconnected tools (e.g., spreadsheets, paper records, and
messaging applications), resulting in inconsistent data, delayed coordination, and
reduced operational efficiency.
                                                                                                         25
         Centralized System ­ A system architecture in which all operational data
and processes are managed within a single integrated platform, enabling
consistent data storage, real-time access, and unified control over administrative
functions.

         Small-Scale Apartment Management ­ Refers to the administrative
operation of residential boarding houses or apartments with limited units, minimal
staff, and constrained digital infrastructure, where management processes are
often hybrid, combining manual and basic digital tools.

         Management Information System (MIS) ­ An information system
framework that processes raw operational data into structured and meaningful
information to support decision-making, coordination, and control within an
organization or operational environment.

         Booking Inquiry ­ A system feature that allows prospective tenants to
submit room availability requests through the platform, which are reviewed and
processed by administrators for approval or scheduling purposes.

         Financial Tracking ­ A system function that records and monitors tenant
payments and operational expenses in a structured digital format, ensuring
accurate financial records and supporting administrative monitoring of income
and expenditures.
                                                                                                         26
         Cash Flow Monitoring ­ The process of generating summarized financial
reports based on recorded transactions, providing insights into income,
expenses, and overall financial movement within the apartment system.

         Issue Ticketing System ­ A structured digital mechanism that allows
tenants to submit maintenance or facility-related concerns, which are then
tracked, assigned, and resolved by administrators through a centralized workflow
system.

         User Authentication ­ A security process that verifies the identity of
users accessing the system to ensure authorized entry and protect sensitive
operational data.

         Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) ­ A security model that restricts
system access based on user roles (e.g., admin, tenant), ensuring that users can
only access functions relevant to their responsibilities.

         Optional Online Payment Feature ­A supplementary system function
that allows tenants to input or simulate digital payment transactions within the
platform, without serving as the primary financial processing mechanism of the
system.

         ISO/IEC 25010 ­ An international software quality standard used to
evaluate system quality based on attributes such as functional suitability,
usability, and security, ensuring structured assessment of system performance.

3.2 Software Methodology

         The researchers will utilize the Agile Software Development Life Cycle
(SDLC) in the development of the Hivelet system. Agile is an iterative and flexible
software development approach that emphasizes continuous improvement,
stakeholder collaboration, and incremental delivery of system components rather
than a rigid, linear process (Pressman & Maxim, 2020).

         As illustrated in Figure 2, the dynamic and resource-constrained nature of
small-scale apartment management requires a development approach that can
adapt to changing operational needs. In this context, Agile is selected as the
most appropriate methodology as it allows continuous interaction with the
landlady and management of the Fe Galang Da Silva Apartment, ensuring that
system features are aligned with actual administrative workflows such as tenant
management, financial tracking, booking coordination, and maintenance
handling.
                                                                                                         31

                          Figure 3. Agile Software Development Life Cycle
         Unlike traditional SDLC models, Agile supports early identification of
system issues through iterative cycles of development and testing. This enables
the researchers to refine system components based on real-time feedback,
resulting in a more reliable, user-centered, and context-appropriate solution.
Furthermore, the Agile approach facilitates progressive evaluation of the system
throughout its development. The system will be assessed using the ISO/IEC
25010 software quality model encompassing functional suitability, performance
efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and
portability. This ensures that the developed system not only meets user
requirements but also adheres to established software quality standards.
                                                                                                         32
         Additionally, the flexibility of Agile allows the system to be continuously
improved, modified, and adapted without compromising performance. This is
particularly important in ensuring that Hivelet remains functional under varying
conditions such as limited infrastructure, unstable internet connectivity, and
evolving user needs.

3.2.1 Requirements

         In this phase, the researchers conduct needfinding through direct
collaboration with the landlady and management of the Fe Galang Da Silva
Apartment. This aligns with the first objective of the study, which focuses on
identifying user needs and operational challenges. Data is gathered through
interviews and observation to examine existing administrative practices, including
the use of manual ledgers, fragmented financial tracking, and unstructured
communication for maintenance requests. These findings reveal inefficiencies
such as inconsistent record-keeping, delayed communication, and lack of
centralized monitoring. Based on these insights, the researchers define the
system requirements, scope, and core functionalities of Hivelet. Emphasis is
placed on the need for a centralized digital platform capable of integrating
booking, financial tracking, and maintenance management within a unified
system.

3.2.2 Design

         During the Design phase, the identified requirements are translated into
system architecture and interface prototypes. The researchers develop
                                                                                                         33
wireframes and UI/UX designs using Figma, covering three user roles: public
users, tenants, and administrators. The system is designed with consideration for
a resource-constrained environment, characterized by limited infrastructure,
unstable internet connectivity, and minimal administrative resources. To address
these constraints, Hivelet is structured as a lightweight Progressive Web
Application (PWA) with optimized performance, simple navigation, and minimal
system requirements.

         Workflows for booking, financial monitoring, and maintenance requests
are mapped and refined through stakeholder feedback to ensure alignment with
real-world operations.

3.2.3 Development

         This phase corresponds to the second objective of the study, which
focuses on developing the system. The researchers implement the Hivelet
system using HTML5, Tailwind CSS, and JavaScript for the front-end interface,
ensuring responsiveness across devices. The back-end is developed using
Node.js and Express.js to manage core functionalities such as booking
processing, financial computations, and maintenance ticket handling. An optional
online payment feature is implemented as an optional integration to simulate or
record digital payments without replacing the primary cash-based financial
workflow. MySQL is used as the database management system to store tenant
records, transaction data, and system logs. The system is developed
                                                                                                         34
incrementally, following Agile principles, allowing continuous refinement based on
feedback and testing results.

3.2.4 Testing

         Aligned with the third objective, this phase focuses on testing and
evaluating the system's performance. The researchers conduct unit testing to
verify individual components such as booking requests, payment tracking, and
notification features. Integration testing is also performed to ensure seamless
interaction between system modules, including booking, financial management,
and maintenance tracking. The optional online payment feature will be tested for
basic functionality, including transaction recording and user interaction. Testing
ensures that the system meets functional requirements, maintains reliability, and
provides a user-friendly experience for both administrators and tenants.

3.2.5 Deployment

         After successful testing, the Hivelet system is deployed to a
university-managed server environment, where the system will be accessible
through a designated URL endpoint for real-world usage and evaluation. A pilot
implementation is conducted with selected users to validate real-world
performance before full deployment. The researchers perform final system
configuration, database migration, and environment setup to ensure smooth
operation.

3.2.6 Review and Maintenance

         This phase complements the evaluation procedure by implementing
refinements and system improvements based on the results gathered from
testing and ISO/IEC 25010 evaluation. Following Agile principles, identified
issues related to functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility,
usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability are addressed through
iterative optimization and maintenance activities.
