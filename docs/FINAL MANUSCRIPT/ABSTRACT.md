Small boarding houses often run on spreadsheets, paper receipts and messages that are not connected
to one another, which the general-purpose property management systems on the market do not fit.
This study designed, developed and evaluated Hivelet, a web-based apartment management system
delivered as a Progressive Web Application, for the Fe Galang Da Silva Boarding House, a 33-unit
property in Legazpi City. The owner's existing practices were analyzed, and her own records,
937 income rows and 1,327 expense allocations, were transferred into the system, which exposed
problems the spreadsheet had hidden. The system was developed in three Agile iterations with Vue,
Express and PostgreSQL, and provides tenant and room management, inquiries about units, financial
tracking with optional GCash payment through Adyen, maintenance requests with notifications,
role-based access control, and installation from the browser on a phone or computer, without an app
store. The GCash payment works end to end in Adyen's test environment, takes no real money and is
not live. The system was pilot tested at the unit and integration, system, and acceptance levels,
and 82 of the 83 tests executed passed (98.8 percent), with no critical defect. Three tenants
completed every task they attempted, 31 of 34 attempts without help, and on the redesigned system a
second round of ten tenants and four prospective tenants completed every task. The system was
evaluated on the eight characteristics of the ISO/IEC 25010:2011 quality model, following the
ISO/IEC 25040 evaluation process, on two kinds of evidence: measures from the tests, and a survey.
The owner, three tenants, a prospective tenant and five technical evaluators rated it 4.59 overall,
Very High Quality, with every characteristic Very High Quality, Portability highest and
Maintainability lowest, and the measurements agreed with the ratings on every characteristic.
Sixteen answers on the redesigned system rated it 4.82, and all 26 answers together 4.61. The
results show that an integrated system can be fitted to a small, cash-based property without
changing how its owner keeps her accounts. The study met its general objective: Hivelet was
designed, developed and evaluated as a web-based apartment management system that improves
administrative workflow integration, communication and operational control at the boarding house.
