# Advertising and campaign art direction

Status: `draft`  
Intervention: `focus`  
Sources: `SRC-AD-CREATIVE-CANON`, `SRC-AD-OPEN-LEARNING`, `SRC-AD-CREATIVE-EVIDENCE`, `SRC-AD-ETHICS-CURRENT`, `SRC-BRAND-CANON`, `SRC-SOURCE-EVIDENCE`, `SRC-PACKAGE-LOCAL-SYNTHESIS`

## Load when

Load when creating, comparing, critiquing, or repairing an advertisement,
persuasive key visual, campaign family, paid-social or display creative,
out-of-home execution, sequential ad set, or cross-placement visual campaign
system. Load when the visual platform, rhetoric, image-copy relation, campaign
invariant, or placement transformation can change the result. Do not load for
media buying, targeting, budget, attribution, copy-only work, campaign strategy
alone, platform-policy lookup, a routine identity application, or one
non-persuasive branded artifact.

## Campaign record and authority

Collect the applicable objective, audience evidence, proposition, offer, product
or service truth, desired action, brand system, mandatory copy and disclosures,
prohibited or unsupported claims, assets and rights, placements and
specifications, exposure conditions, sequence and flight context, accessibility
requirements, KPI owner, approval authority, and unknowns. Mark supplied
strategy as approved, observed, inferred, proposed, or unresolved. Do not invent
positioning, audience insight, product performance, endorsement, scarcity,
comparison, or outcome evidence.

Missing inputs block only work that depends on them. For a bounded draft or
repair, preserve accepted inputs and continue unaffected authorised work;
identify unresolved assumptions as proposals, not approved campaign facts.
Do not invent claims or use assets without required permission even in drafts.
Release additionally requires applicable claim, rights, disclosure, placement
and owner approvals. An unrelated KPI or flight decision does not block a
local visual repair; request it only when it changes that task or its acceptance.

Record the art direction as:

`approved proposition | campaign thesis | visual rhetorical operation |
image-copy relation | recognition cue | message or offer takeout | action and
disclosure path | invariant | controlled variable | placement transformation |
sequence role | likely counter-reading | evidence | owner/status`.

Brand supplies the identity constraints. Strategy or marketing supplies the
objective, audience, proposition, offer, positioning, and success metric.
The wording owner supplies final wording. This leaf does not silently repair
any of those inputs.

## Generate and decide

1. Translate the approved proposition into one visual campaign thesis. State
   how a viewer recognizes the category, brand or product, understands the
   intended takeout, and reaches the action or disclosure. Do not substitute a
   mood adjective or visual trend for that relation.
2. Explore materially different persuasive mechanisms. Options may include
   demonstration, consequence, contrast, substitution, exaggeration declared
   as such, visual analogy, juxtaposition, sequence, reveal, testimony with
   authority, product evidence, social context, typographic proposition, or an
   owned brand behaviour. A changed colour, crop, headline position, or style is
   not a different mechanism.
3. Make the image-copy relation functional. Image and words can identify,
   complete, constrain, contradict, demonstrate, qualify, sequence, or
   intentionally repeat each other. Record the required inference and the
   plausible unintended reading created by adjacency, crop, scale, order, and
   emphasis.
4. Establish a campaign invariant strong enough to connect placements without
   cloning one layout. The invariant can be a rhetorical relation, product
   behaviour, image grammar, framing device, sequence, typographic behaviour,
   compositional move, or another recognisable mechanism. Define which parts
   may vary and why.
5. Design attention as an entry condition, not the outcome. Ensure the entry
   leads to ad recognition, brand or product identification, proposition or
   offer takeout, and the appropriate action or disclosure. A striking image
   that is misattributed or communicates the wrong result fails the campaign.
6. Use real product, price, claim, qualifier, and disclosure content. Place
   material qualifications where the overall impression can use them. Small
   text cannot reverse a dominant unsupported visual implication.
7. Recompose for each real placement. Account for exposure time, viewing
   distance, ratio, crop, interface chrome, sequence, animation, sound-off,
   motion-off, safe areas, truncation, locale, density, and interaction. Do not
   treat placement-native work as mechanical resizing.
8. Preserve brand linkage without forcing every brand element to compete.
   Decide when recognition comes from the mark, product, distinctive asset,
   colour relation, type, image language, sound, or recurring campaign device.
   Verify that campaign expression does not become an unauthorised new identity.
9. Build variants around declared questions. Change one causal variable when a
   comparison is intended to learn from performance. Keep proposition,
   audience, placement, exposure, metric window, and unrelated design variables
   stable unless the test explicitly studies their interaction.
10. Compare concepts at equal fidelity with exact content and representative
    placements. Evaluate originality and appropriateness separately. Select by
    gates, likely takeout, brand linkage, placement survival, ethical risk,
    evidence quality, and the uncertainty most likely to reverse the decision.

Treat a button-shaped carrier as an action only when an action exists. If a
pill, tab or control-like shape instead labels a route, theme or proposition,
name that role and check whether the visual affordance falsely promises
interaction. Repair the carrier or the action hierarchy, not just its colour.

## Test the carrier and its counter-reading

Suppose an explicitly fictional brief authorises “Share the equipment”. A
demonstration can show one identifiable object handed from one person to
another; an analogy might show separate paths meeting at that object. Compare
what the image actually communicates without the headline, then restore the
words and inspect what they clarify. The analogy may accidentally imply shared
ownership or universal availability; repair or qualify it if those claims are
not supplied. A literal object and booking instruction can outperform the
analogy for an immediate task. These original examples explain a comparison;
they do not prove persuasion, audience response or the fictional proposition.

## Critique failure signatures and causes

- **Eye-catching but unidentifiable.** The image or effect wins attention while
  product, advertiser, or category remains unclear. Repair the recognition
  chain, not merely logo size.
- **Memorable execution, wrong takeout.** Visual rhetoric implies a different
  benefit, audience, condition, comparison, or consequence than the approved
  proposition. Correct the dominant relation before adding explanatory copy.
- **Brand appears only as a signature.** The campaign mechanism and identity
  never interact, so the same ad can take any logo. Build an owned relation
  without turning the identity into decoration.
- **Brand overwhelms the proposition.** Mandatory assets are repeated or enlarged
  until message and evidence disappear. Restore the task hierarchy while
  remaining conformant.
- **Image and copy duplicate or cancel.** Both explain the same literal content,
  or one creates a counter-reading the other cannot repair. Reassign their
  rhetorical jobs.
- **Campaign is a mood-board bundle.** Palette, type, grain, collage, or trend
  cues supply coherence but no repeatable persuasive mechanism. Define the
  campaign invariant and rebuild executions from it.
- **Every format is a crop of the hero.** Product, action, qualifier, sequence,
  or focal relation is lost in actual placements. Recompose from invariant and
  priority rather than preserving coordinates.
- **Variant set cannot teach.** Multiple variables, messages, audiences, and
  placements change together. Separate creative exploration from a controlled
  performance comparison.
- **Complexity is treated as the defect.** Dense structure may be purposeful,
  while low structure can still be cluttered. Localise competing entry points,
  feature clutter, weak grouping, or false hierarchy instead of demanding
  universal simplicity.
- **Disclosure is technically present but visually defeated.** Placement,
  duration, contrast, motion, crop, or dominant imagery prevents the qualifier
  from affecting the whole impression. Stop release and return the claim or
  treatment to the responsible authority.
- **Performance claim exceeds evidence.** Attention, clicks, liking, or one
  variant result is reported as persuasion, brand lift, conversion cause, or
  future performance. Narrow the claim and preserve confounds.

For each finding name the placement and frame, visual observation, likely
takeout or action effect, evidence, confirmed or inferred cause, governing
owner, smallest repair, campaign strengths to preserve, affected variants,
required recheck, and unknowns. Separate defect, tradeoff, attributed
preference, intentional exception, policy or legal risk, and unverified
performance.

## Repair and regression

Repair the earliest campaign cause. Correct proposition mapping before visual
hierarchy, rhetoric before treatment, image-copy relation before placement,
invariant before variants, and source claim before disclosure styling. Return
unsupported strategy, claim, offer, or wording to its owner rather than
inventing a design fix.

Preserve approved proposition, brand invariants, exact content, valid campaign
device, strong product evidence, deliberate tone, working placement-specific
variation, and authorised exceptions. Rebuild only dependent executions, then
inspect the whole family. Recheck recognition, takeout, action and disclosure
in each representative placement, crop, sequence, sound state, motion state,
locale, and access route that applies.

For a controlled comparison, freeze the hypothesis, changed variable, control,
population, placement, delivery conditions, metric, window, stop rule, and
confounds before results. A design repair and a performance experiment are
different records. Do not optimize away brand, disclosure, access, or truth to
win a local metric.

## Rules and deliberate exceptions

Binding constraints include approved proposition and offer, product truth,
incumbent identity, required copy and disclosures, rights and participant
conditions, accessibility duties, current placement requirements, and release
authority. Attention hierarchy, brand linkage, complementary image-copy roles,
campaign invariants, and placement-native recomposition are mechanisms to
test, not a recipe for loud simplicity.

An understated ad, long-copy execution, ambiguous reveal, dense retail layout,
logo-light teaser, hard crop, visual pun, repetition, mixed-media system,
placement-specific departure, or intentionally disruptive composition can be
valid when the audience can recover the advertiser and intended takeout at the
required point, protected truth and disclosure survive, the campaign relation
remains coherent, and a named test can falsify the choice. Creative licence
cannot manufacture evidence, imitate a protected identity, exploit a person
without authority, hide material conditions, or turn an unsupported implication
into a permissible claim.

## Proof, ownership, and claim ceiling

Evidence can include the frozen brief and source claims, same-content mechanism
roughs, visual-rhetoric and counter-reading record, image-copy map, campaign
invariant sheet, exact placement specifications, placement-native renders,
sequence and sound-state views, disclosure inspection, brand conformance record,
asset and participant status, controlled comparison preregistration, observed
results, repair history, and approval state. Inspect the real ad in its
placement context, not only an isolated presentation board.

This leaf owns the visual creative platform, proposition-to-visual mapping,
visual rhetoric, functional image-copy relation, campaign invariants,
controlled visual variables, placement-native variants, and creative test
contract. Brand owns identity and governance. Strategy and marketing own
objective, audience, proposition, offer, positioning, and KPI. The wording owner
owns wording. Media owners control targeting, spend, delivery, attribution, and
experiment operations. Source, Rights, Privacy, legal, regulatory, and current
platform authorities own claims, permission, disclosure, and policy acceptance.
Production owns delivered artifacts.

Claim `placement-rendered`, `campaign-family-inspected`, `brand-conformance
checked`, `whole-impression risk flagged`, or the exact observed comparison
result only when supported. Do not claim persuasion, memorability, brand lift,
conversion, causality, future performance, platform approval, legal compliance,
ethical acceptance, rights clearance, or market effectiveness from a creative
render or expert critique.
