## Load when

Load when a decision affects Corporate Design/corporate visual identity,
identity recognition, brand architecture, incumbent visual-identity
conformance, cross-touchpoint requirements, mark/lockup configuration roles,
icon/pictogram/symbol families, controlled variation, template or campaign
inheritance, governance, migration, or lifecycle. An authored icon family or
stroke system also selects this owner outside identity work. Do not load for generic
Corporate Identity strategy, behaviour, culture, naming or verbal identity;
standalone mark-form creation; persuasive campaign art direction; one isolated
supplied colour/type/icon/layout choice; or a medium template with no identity-
system consequence.

## Operation and incumbent relation

Resolve one package mode first: `generate`, `critique` or `repair`.
Style direction remains a domain within those modes. Then record one identity operation:

- `create`: no applicable identity exists or redesign is authorised;
- `apply`: use an incumbent identity without redefining it;
- `audit`: compare artifacts with current authority without editing either;
- `evolve`: preserve, retire or migrate equity under explicit authority;
- `document`: encode accepted decisions without inventing missing ones.

Separately record `incumbent relation: conform | authorised change | no
applicable incumbent | unresolved`. `No applicable incumbent` needs an explicit
owner statement or documented authority search; missing files are not evidence.
Unknown currentness, authority, scope or equal-rank contradiction is
`unresolved` and blocks non-provisional identity mutation. `Conform` constrains
any package mode; it is not a fifth mode or a low-creativity setting.

## Inputs and authority

Scope the input and system inventories below to the current identity operation
and affected touchpoints. Record material fields, preserving applicable
incumbent authority, exact content, rights and approval constraints; do not
reopen unrelated settled fields.

Record the organisation/product/campaign and intended audience; approved
strategy, name, message and verbal assets; incumbent identity owner and source
files; touchpoints, languages, partners and environments; recognition or user
evidence; rights/trademark status; accessibility and production constraints;
launch, transition, expiry and approval authority. Mark each input as approved,
observed, inferred, proposed or unknown. Do not invent positioning, values,
audience research, legal clearance or recognition evidence to fill a brief.

Inventory the system: mark and wordmark, lockups, typography, colour, imagery,
graphic devices, pattern, motion, sound if relevant, icon/pictogram/symbol
families, templates, data/diagram language, spacing/layout behaviours, co-brand
rules and asset variants. Separate canonical assets from derivatives and
temporary campaign layers.

For every applicable incumbent rule record:

`rule | source/version/location | authority/owner | scope |
mandatory/recommended/open | invariant | allowed variation |
forbidden mutation | precedence | exception owner/expiry | proof state`.

Inspect the task's actual authority rather than assuming one universal source
hierarchy. A useful search order is owner instruction, current approved manual,
canonical assets/templates, tokens/libraries, approved applications, observed
production and finally explicit inference. A lower source cannot silently
override a higher one; equal-rank conflict stays unresolved. Current mandatory
CI outranks an aesthetically preferable alternative. Accessibility, law,
rights, safety, provider constraints and unknown authority are not aesthetic
exceptions.

For architecture, record each entity, audience-facing role, parent relation,
naming authority, identity freedom, endorsement, partner weight, touchpoints,
migration and exception. `Branded house`, `endorsed`, `subbrand`, `house of
brands` and `hybrid` are analysis options, not templates. Do not create a logo
for every organisational label by default.

## Generate or decide

1. State one visual-identity thesis that connects the approved strategy to a
   perceivable behaviour. It must guide a decision, not repeat adjectives such
   as bold, modern or premium.
2. Define **invariants** that carry recognition and meaning, **controlled
   variables** that create range, and **forbidden mutations** that break the
   system. Rank element priority so a touchpoint can remain recognisable when
   size, colour, image, motion or copy is unavailable.
3. Specify each element by role and relationships, not by a gallery of polished
   examples. Test how type, colour, imagery, composition, graphic devices and
   voice combine; avoid making every component distinctive at once.
4. For marks and lockups, define their architecture role, required and approved
   configurations, status, inheritance, contexts, partner hierarchy,
   localisation, migration and failure states. The Mark expert owns formal
   construction, figure/ground, custom wordmark relations and optical variants;
   strict use of a canonical mark does not load it. Do not derive universal
   clear-space or minimum-size numbers from another brand.
5. For icons, pictograms and symbols, define semantic inventory, construction
   grammar, stroke/fill/terminal/corner logic, optical size, bounding behaviour,
   detail/reduction levels, state variants and naming. Similar geometry must not
   collapse distinct meanings; matching boxes must not replace optical balance.
6. Govern templates through identity invariants, allowed roles, controlled
   variation, versions, approvals, migration and expiry. Fixed Media owns page,
   spread, slide, document and fold structure; UI owns interactive component/
   pattern structure; Web owns responsive transformation; Packaging owns pack
   panel/SKU structure. The medium owner hands an accepted structure to Brand
   for governance. Encode resolved decisions into tokens/components only after
   those relations are designed.
7. Define campaign inheritance explicitly: what remains from the parent
   identity, what may temporarily change, which identity invariants/variants
   are allowed, who owns them, and when they expire. Advertising Art Direction
   owns the persuasive creative platform, visual rhetoric, image-copy relation
   and placement-native campaign variants. A campaign is not one hero layout
   cloned into every ratio or a late logo applied to unrelated creative.
8. Create the smallest authoritative asset set and registry that can generate
   required derivatives. Include version, status, owner, source, allowed use,
   replacement/migration and deprecation state.

## Stroke-family procedure

1. Define `role/family | intended size | weight | caps/joins | scaling policy`
   before extending the family. Reuse these decisions for peers.
2. Compare same-role/same-size peers for consistent apparent weight beside their
   typography and actual background. Measure effective widths after transforms;
   matching source attributes alone do not prove consistency.
3. Permit different weights for hierarchy, expressive grammar, optical size or
   production. Name the purpose and inspect the effect; unexplained peer drift
   is a repair signal. Different roles need not share one width.
   If weight carries hierarchy, its distinction must be visible at target size
   and background; a numerical difference is insufficient. Optical compensation
   may use unequal widths for equal apparent weight.
4. Inspect the compound motif: both outer junctions, internal gaps, caps, counters
   and neighbouring fills/clips. Equal weights do not prove coherent connections;
   check intended overlaps and separations, including asymmetric ones. Repair
   the family or paint order before local nudges; recheck after weight or scale
   changes at the smallest required output.

This is a synthesis of [IBM icon guidance](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/iconography/ui-icons/design/),
[IBM illustration guidance](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/illustration/line-style/design/)
and [Material Symbols](https://developers.google.com/fonts/docs/material_symbols).
Their specific values are system conventions, not universal pixel rules.

## Build a family rather than matching boxes

At consolidation or inspection, judge icon form first: recognisable meaning,
proportions, apparent weight, drawing and family consistency, using the stroke
procedure above. Then judge placement, enclosure, background and accents
separately. A useful icon function does not validate its drawing or surrounding
treatment. Preserve useful recognition cues while repairing the actual cause.

The [sewing-label source](../examples/icon-treatment/comparison.svg) and
[render](../examples/icon-treatment/comparison.png) compare original tiles (A),
unchanged icons without pill/accent (B), and revised forms with original
treatment (C). Compare A–B for treatment and A–C for drawing; B–C changes both.
Tiles are regrouped for comparison. No variant is an automatic winner; neither
ellipses nor accents are inherently wrong. Use only for this open distinction.

Compare a compact, a wide and a diagonal member at their actual use size.
Inspect apparent mass and centre, counter size, terminal/corner treatment and
detail survival beside the unchanged incumbent when one applies. Equal bounds
can make a sparse diagonal feel smaller than a filled square; compare extending
its optical extent with changing stroke weight, rather than scaling every
member identically. Preserve each symbol's distinct meaning.

For a pattern, separate the motif from the repeat operation. A fictional
paper-strip device might preserve one cut angle and the relation of two edges
while translation, offset and spacing vary. Draw adjoining repeats, not only
one tile: inspect accidental stripes, holes, seam accents and the smallest gap.
Then inspect the actual cropped surface. Density and edge behavior belong to
the identity's job; a deliberate broken repeat or cropped motif may be correct.
Keep a useful incumbent grammar instead of importing another brand's keyline,
stroke width or mandated rhythm. These comparisons are original working aids,
not optical equations or universal family counts.

## Critique: failure signatures and causes

- **Recognisable only in the full hero composition:** no ranked invariants or
  reduced-state strategy.
- **Consistent but generic:** uniform styling exists without a subject-specific
  thesis or distinctive relationship. If identity evolution is open, choose an
  owned relation from verified subject evidence: product geometry, process
  rhythm, place, word behaviour or material. Rank it as an invariant, then hide
  the loudest borrowed cue and inspect whether the relationship survives.
  Preserve an accepted identity; an audit finding does not authorise redesign.
- **Distinctive but unusable:** signature elements fail small, one-colour,
  dense, localised, accessible, co-brand or low-production contexts.
- **Mark family drifts:** lockups were redrawn from derivatives, optical
  corrections are inconsistent, or canonical assets/variants lack ownership.
- **Icon family looks related but communicates poorly:** visual grammar outran
  semantic inventory, accessible naming, state meaning or reduction tests.
- **Templates either clone or fragment:** variables and invariants are not
  separated, content ranges are untested, or local teams patch examples rather
  than use governed rules.
- **Campaign overwhelms or disappears into the parent:** inheritance and expiry
  are unspecified.
- **A token library exists but decisions remain contradictory:** implementation
  encoded unresolved design rather than a coherent system.
- **Old and new assets coexist silently:** no version, migration, retirement or
  downstream consumer record.
- **Attractive but nonconforming:** a local improvement violates a current
  mandatory rule or uses an unapproved derivative. Restore the canonical rule
  or request an authorised exception; never silently redefine the CI.
- **Authority laundering:** absent, stale or contradictory sources were treated
  as permission. Set the relation to `unresolved`, preserve the conflict and
  stop non-provisional mutation.

For each finding name the affected touchpoints, observation, likely effect,
root owner, confidence, smallest system-level repair, assets that must be
rebuilt, strengths to preserve and evidence still absent. Separate a defect
from a tradeoff, attributed preference, intentional exception and unverified
recognition claim.

## Repair and regression

Change the canonical owner before patching derivatives. Repair an invariant,
variable definition, element grammar, template rule, asset registry or
governance decision, then regenerate only affected outputs. Preserve approved
strategy, recognisable relationships, correct semantics, working touchpoints
and valid local exceptions. Never “make everything consistent” by deleting the
variation that lets the system fit real content and media.

Regression-test the changed system with real content across one-colour,
reversed, small, low-resolution, dense, sparse, long/short copy, localised,
co-brand, campaign, template, UI, print and motion contexts that actually
apply. Compare canonical and migrated assets, and invalidate screenshots or
guidance made from superseded versions.

## Rules and exceptions

Binding constraints are approved names/content/assets, incumbent authority,
rights and partner agreements, exact semantic meaning, accessibility duties,
required destinations and release state. Evidence-bounded claims about
recognition, distinctiveness, usability or preference require the relevant
audience and task. Conventions - stable signatures, controlled variation,
canonical sources, clear governance - are means, not a guarantee of a strong
identity. Starting heuristics such as few core elements, one master asset or a
limited palette must yield to actual roles and production needs.

A temporary campaign mutation, responsive lockup, simplified mark, different
small-size icon, local-language composition, accessible replacement or
production-specific variant may break a visual rule when the owner, scope,
purpose, compensation, proof and expiry are recorded. An exception without a
lifecycle becomes accidental system drift.

## Proof, ownership and claim ceiling

Inspect the system, not only a mood board: render the stress contexts above
with real content and compare variants side by side. Validate source assets and
derivative identity, icon semantics and reduction, template content pressure,
co-brand order, accessibility states, version/migration records and the actual
delivery formats. Use audience recognition or comprehension studies only for
their stated population and task.

Brand owns cross-touchpoint visual grammar, identity architecture, required and
approved mark/lockup configurations, icon-family grammar, controlled variation,
visual conformance and governance. The Mark expert owns identity-mark form and
optical variants. UI Workflow owns a UI icon's semantic job, state and
accessible name; the implementation owner implements the incumbent system.
Information specialists own map/chart/diagram symbol semantics. Culture owns
sensitive-symbol authority. Asset Rights and counsel determine use and legal
clearance; strategy and naming remain with their approved owners.

Deterministic conformance may identify foreign tokens/colours, wrong files or
weights, distorted canonical assets, missing mandatory variants and exact-name
failures. Visual judgment still owns hierarchy, optical relations, image
language and controlled variation. Every conformance finding cites the actual
rule version and locator; do not hide different severities behind one brand
score.

Do not claim trademark clearance, originality, market distinctiveness,
recognition, accessibility, rollout adoption or production readiness from a
coherent render. Report exact assets, contexts and evidence inspected, and keep
unknown authority or rights as a release blocker rather than a design guess.
