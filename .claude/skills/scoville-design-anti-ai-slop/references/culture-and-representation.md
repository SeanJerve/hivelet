# Culture and representation

## Load when

Load when the work uses or depicts a living culture, community or identity;
sacred, secret, ceremonial, funerary, political or contested material; a flag,
emblem or protest symbol; a disputed boundary or place name; or when stereotype,
appropriation, tokenism, exoticization, erasure, cultural protocol, attribution
or benefit can change the decision. Do not load merely because a brief says
`global`, names a historical style with no living-authority issue, or requests
ordinary localisation. Jurisdiction is a modifier after this route is selected,
not an activation signal by itself.

## Inputs, authority and formal variables

Record the exact material and intended transformation; speaker/publisher;
communication purpose; audience; medium and placement; commercial, editorial or
community context; distribution place, territory and duration; named people,
community and affected parties; source, date and historical context; language,
script, endonym/exonym, boundary and symbol version; public, restricted, sacred,
secret or contested state; and current rights, protocol, confidentiality,
attribution and benefit conditions.

Build an authority map. Distinguish custodian or rights holder, a participant
speaking for themself, community-selected representative, language or names
authority, commissioned collaborator, publisher, and legal, privacy,
safeguarding or production owner. For each record who selected them, mandate,
material they may decide, review stage, conditions, dissent, expiry or withdrawal
point, and unresolved questions. Visibility, membership, employment, expertise,
museum possession or commissioner status does not automatically grant authority
for a whole community or restricted material.

For a place-name or boundary decision also record map/data version, feature ID,
publisher jurisdiction, claimant positions, portrayal instruction, protected
label/line/legend/note, alternate editions and accountable geographic authority.

## Generate or decide

1. State the representational job in subject-specific terms: who is acting,
   speaking, making, deciding or benefiting, and what context must remain. Do not
   use identity as atmosphere or proof of virtue.
2. Locate the relevant authority before selecting or transforming consequential
   material. Consult early enough to change premise, source, access,
   compensation, credit, distribution or release; provide exact intended crops,
   captions, channels and adaptations rather than asking for broad approval.
3. Prefer community-authored or explicitly authorised material when living or
   restricted knowledge is load-bearing. Preserve names, diacritics, scripts,
   attribution, protocols and dissent through every version.
4. Test every person, motif, object, colour, pattern, landscape, costume,
   archive, juxtaposition and omission against the actual subject. Replace
   interchangeable cultural shorthand with specific role, source, relationship,
   time, place and authorship.
5. For sacred, secret, ceremonial or credible restricted material, stop creation
   or publication while authority or access is unknown. Use verified public
   material, an authorised contribution, a nonrepresentational structure or
   removal as the safe fallback.
6. For political symbols and contested maps/names, document the exact scoped
   publisher position. Do not let a basemap default, code list, search result,
   colour fill or generic disclaimer silently decide sovereignty or endorsement.

## Critique: failure signatures and causes

- **Tokenism:** one person or motif functions only as demographic proof,
  ornament, victim or helper; the brief supplied no individual role or agency.
- **Stereotype, including a positive one:** competence, temperament, family
  role, spirituality or body is predetermined; casting and image selection
  followed a category rather than the actual subject.
- **Exoticization:** costume, pattern, landscape, food, gesture or spirituality
  intensifies difference while ordinary work, humour, expertise and conflict
  disappear; mood keywords replaced sourced context.
- **Erasure:** dominant archive names, language, history or boundary appears
  neutral while community authors, endonyms, dissent or contested status is
  omitted; publisher defaults were mistaken for authority.
- **Timeless cultural collage:** different peoples, regions, periods or
  practices are fused into one aesthetic; style markers were gathered without
  provenance or temporal relation.
- **Restricted material used as decoration:** online or museum availability was
  mistaken for permission; custodian, audience, transformation or benefit is
  unknown.
- **Political or cartographic normalisation:** symbol, border, label, colour,
  crop or missing legend implies endorsement or settled status; the exact
  version, audience and authority were never recorded.
- **Consultation theatre:** a late reviewer sees only a polished mockup, has no
  decision rights, or dissent disappears; review could not change the work.

Localise the asset, crop, caption, role, motif, hierarchy, omission, boundary or
label. State the likely representational effect, named or missing authority,
cause as confirmed/inferred/unknown, and distribution consequence. The phrase
“may be insensitive” without this evidence is not a finding.

## Smallest repair and regression

Repair the failed authority or representation relation: clarify a person's
specific role; restore name, authorship, language, context, disputed status or
dissent; replace an unsourced marker; change crop/caption/hierarchy; narrow use;
commission an authorised contributor; obtain a scoped decision; issue a local
edition; or withhold the material. Stylization, parody, a disclaimer or
“inspired by” does not cure an authority gap.

Preserve the valid communication thesis, participant agency, authorised public
material, factual and historical context, required script/diacritics, rights,
credit, benefit and confidentiality. Reinspect every crop, translation, map
scale, responsive state, print version, caption, alt text, metadata field and
downstream derivative; a repair must not erase the group, hide a dispute or
expose restricted context elsewhere.

## Rules and exceptions

Binding constraints are named authority and mandate, restricted or confidential
status, supplied facts, approved source and transformation, participant terms,
publisher position, attribution/benefit, and current jurisdiction or contract
when applicable. Historical convention, broad cultural familiarity, public
display, model fluency and visual plausibility are never cultural clearance.

A quotation, critique, documentary record, community-led revival, parody,
public-interest use or unavoidable contested label can be justified only when
purpose, necessity, authority, exact material, alternatives, audience, harm,
context, dissent, rights, distribution and expiry are recorded. Preserve the
exception as scoped and unresolved where owners disagree; do not convert it
into a universal approval.

## Proof, ownership and claim ceiling

Inspect the authority record, source material, consulted context and final
render together. Verify protected names, scripts, boundaries, notes, credits,
conditions and restrictions in every delivered form. Record reviewer mandate,
decision, conditions, dissent, version, date and next review; test the artifact
with affected or qualified authorities when consequences are material.

This module owns representational framing, authority boundary, cultural
protocol and contested place/symbol context. Image owners control treatment;
map and source owners control their evidence; rights owners record permission;
affected communities and accountable authorities decide cultural use. Report
only the inspected decision and scope. Never claim authenticity, neutrality,
community consensus, cultural safety, consent, legality or universal permission
from a model review or one consultation.
