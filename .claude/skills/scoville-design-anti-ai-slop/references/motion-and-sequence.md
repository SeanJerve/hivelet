## Load when

Load when time, state change, pacing, attention transfer, continuity, kinetic
type, storyboard, loop, interaction motion, or a reduced/static equivalent can
change meaning or quality. Load for temporal critique or repair, even when the
implementation already exists. A sequence of authored still states selects
this module when state change, persistent identity, transition meaning or
continuity remains open, even if the requested output itself is static. Do not
load merely for selecting or ordering already independent still images with no
state/continuity relation, settled framework-token implementation, codec/export
work without temporal judgment, or a static artifact merely described as
dynamic.

## Inputs and formal variables

Record only open relevant fields; otherwise use the Core minimal record:

- `J`: message or task, audience, consequence, motion mode (linear, looped,
  interactive or live), frequency, platform and removal test;
- `S`: canonical stable states, persistent identities, start/end conditions,
  triggers, feedback, focus/reading order, and essential state information;
- `B`: beats with content, primary/supporting/quiet role, entry, readable hold,
  emphasis, exit, bridge, overlap and settled state;
- `C`: exact copy, language/script, data, imagery, audio/caption relation,
  documentary status, protected subject/context and permitted transforms;
- `T`: duration constraint, aspect/dimensions, safe areas, viewing distance,
  input modes, first/final/poster/loop frames and destination variants;
- `R`: interruption, cancel, reverse, replay, repeated activation, background/
  offscreen behaviour, reduced/static requirements, control and flashing risk;
- `E`: storyboard, runtime, frame, access/safety, performance, export and human
  evidence, each kept separate.

## Generate and decide

1. **Write a motion thesis.** State what changes, why, what remains stable and
   how the change serves `J`. Define a focal sequence and quiet support. Remove
   motion that can disappear without loss of meaning, orientation, feedback or
   character unless its expressive job is explicit and defensible.
   Derive a candidate thesis from actual subject change: process, state
   transition, physical behaviour or data trend. Without change or another
   justified temporal job, motion is a candidate for removal, not a requirement.
2. **Design states before transitions.** Make every required start, intermediate
   commitment and end state legible as a composition. Motion cannot repair weak
   hierarchy, type, spacing, crop or subject recognition in the stable frames.
3. **Build temporal hierarchy from real content.** Sequence attention and causal
   order; protect reading holds and stillness. Set duration from copy, language,
   task, distance, frequency and target testing - not universal timing, stagger,
   spring, frame-rate or layer-count recipes.
4. **Choose a continuity spine.** Preserve identity through spatial, semantic,
   causal, temporal or perceptual relations. Specify origin, path, occlusion,
   transformation, velocity character and destination. A deliberate cut or
   discontinuity is valid when another orientation cue preserves the intended
   relation.
5. **Keep state independent of effect.** Define behaviour when motion is skipped,
   interrupted, cancelled, reversed, retriggered or loses its target. Interactive
   motion samples canonical state; it must not own, delay or corrupt it.
6. **Give loops an ending model.** Declare job, seam/reset, count or control,
   visibility behaviour, persistent state, replay, first/final/poster frames and
   reduced/static result. Resolved feedback must not imply endless progress.
7. **Treat kinetic type as typography over time.** Test exact shaping, glyphs,
   fallback, line breaks, punctuation, captions, safe areas and localisation.
   Provide stable readable states; do not animate per glyph or reuse Latin
   timing merely because a tool permits it.
8. **Author reduced and static equivalents from the first storyboard.** Preserve
   essential state, order, relation, cause, value, feedback and conclusion.
   Replacement may be instant update, bounded cue, key frames, small multiples,
   labels or transcript; a frozen first/last frame or slow fade is not
   automatically equivalent.

## Compare the movement and the hold separately

Sketch the start and destination first. For an object settling into a known
place, compare a path that slows near arrival with a constant-speed move. For
departure, compare how quickly the object yields attention to what remains.
For a measured progression, constant speed may communicate more honestly than
an expressive acceleration. These are role-dependent candidates, not universal
entry/exit curves. A justified expressive overshoot is not banned outside an
incumbent system that disallows it.

Use the same object, distance and states for the initial curve comparison.
Then vary duration if needed; a larger journey and a frequently repeated cue
may need different treatment. Distinguish travel time from the hold after the
message becomes readable. For an original sequence such as a folded sheet
opening into a map, inspect when orientation becomes clear and protect the
subsequent reading interval instead of stretching every transition.

Play the whole study, interrupt it and inspect its reduced/static equivalent.
Judge the next beat from the actual settled state, not from a timeline label.
Keep stillness when it already carries the job; no movement, timing range or
easing family is compulsory merely because this comparison is available.

## Critique: failure signatures and causes

| Failure signature | Likely parent cause to test |
| --- | --- |
| Everything moves and nothing leads | no temporal hierarchy, competing peaks, no protected hold or stillness |
| Sequence feels like generic slides or a template site | fade-up stagger, parallax or hover lift repeated everywhere; interchangeable frames; no subject thesis or continuity carrier |
| Object teleports or duplicates | persistent identity, origin/path/destination or canonical state is unresolved |
| Back, cancel or repeated input breaks state | animation owns state, cannot reverse/interrupt, or queues stale targets |
| Text exists but cannot be read | hold ignores attention arrival, shaping, line breaks, language, safe area or final renderer |
| Loop distracts or signals false progress | no singular job, stop/control, seam, settled state or repeated-use test |
| Reduced mode loses meaning | displacement carried state/relation; replacement hides content or freezes an intermediate state |
| Motion is smooth but weak | runtime metric was mistaken for hierarchy, pacing, consequence or subject fit |

Repeated effects can legitimately carry state, order or attention transfer.
Test that job before removing them; repair the temporal relation, not its fashion.

Timecode findings and name likely effect, cause as confirmed/inferred/unknown,
severity/confidence, smallest temporal repair, preserved strengths and missing
evidence. A screenshot cannot prove pacing, interruption, flashing, reduced
equivalence, performance or export.

## Smallest repair, preservation, and regression

Freeze protected content and valid stable states. Diagnose: thesis/state -> beat
order and hierarchy -> continuity/identity -> hold/pacing -> path/transformation
-> curve/local polish. Repair the earliest temporal cause rather than slowing
everything or adding transitions. Preserve correct data, readable type,
recognisable imagery, successful still compositions, feedback, sequence logic
and intended character. Retest start/mid/end, interrupt, cancel, reverse,
retrigger, full loop and reduced/static paths. Revert a repair that changes
state truth, blocks input/focus, creates a flash risk, loses localisation,
breaks another aspect ratio, or only moves the defect to a transition midpoint.

## Rule classes and exceptions

Protected content/state, applicable access and safety requirements, participant
conditions and destination contracts are binding. Craft principles about
attention, continuity and holds are contextual; timing, easing, path, bounce,
cut, fade, parallax, jitter or physical plausibility are never universal quality
rules.

For web scope, apply criteria exactly: WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 Level A requires a
pause, stop or hide mechanism for moving, blinking or scrolling information
that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and appears alongside
other content, unless essential; automatically updating content has a separate
condition. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1 Level A permits no more than three flashes in any
one-second period unless below the general and red-flash thresholds; analyze
the final delivered variants at the largest relevant viewing condition. A
warning, off switch or named style does not make an initially unsafe flash
safe. SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions is Level AAA, not a universal AA
rule; motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled unless
essential to function or information.

`prefers-reduced-motion` is one user request, not full accessibility evidence;
`no-preference` is not consent to maximal motion. A stronger product baseline
may be adopted but must be labelled product policy. Abrupt, nonphysical,
discontinuous, jittered or expressive motion remains valid only with stable
meaning/readability, a compensating structure, reduced equivalent, exact
safety scope, target proof and a falsifier.

## Proof, ownership, and claim ceiling

Inspect storyboard and normal-speed target playback, then slow play, sampled
frames and transition midpoints. Capture stable states, readable holds,
first/final/poster frames and two full loop cycles. Exercise interrupt, cancel,
reverse, retrigger, background/offscreen and all intended inputs; compare normal,
reduced and static outputs for information/state parity. Where applicable run
exact flash analysis on final encodes and import/play the delivered variant.
For a static storyboard or state row, also check its repeated internal rhythm,
both outside endpoints against any shared span, and each nested status or action
surface against its content. Equal panel gaps cannot close an external-anchor
or local-content-fit relation.

Performance requires target runtime traces under representative load; source
properties, a preview or apparent smoothness are not proof.

Motion owns temporal thesis, states, hierarchy, continuity and reduced/static
intent. UI/Code owns live state, focus/input behaviour, cancellation and runtime
performance; Production owns render, codec, colour, audio, captions and final
delivery evidence; content/data/participant owners retain truth and permission.
Motion evidence does not prove comprehension, vestibular safety, WCAG
conformance, runtime performance, script readability, production readiness or
cross-platform behaviour beyond the exact tested conditions.
