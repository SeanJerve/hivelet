# Brief framing and criteria

Status: `draft`  
Intervention: `focus`  
Sources: `SRC-BRIEF-HCD`, `SRC-FOUNDATION-GRAPHIC`, `SRC-PROFESSIONAL-SCOPE`, `SRC-PACKAGE-LOCAL-SYNTHESIS`, `SRC-COMMUNICATION-CANON`

## Load when

Load when a required brief field is missing, contradictory, weakly authorised,
or must become explicit content, freedom, success, selection, or approval
criteria; also load for an open communication concern such as misreading risk
or modality relation, even when all brief fields were supplied. Do not load
for a complete settled brief with no open communication concern, ordinary restatement,
concept generation, visual treatment, or user research itself.

## Frame record

Use the Core minimal record unless open fields require expansion. For a material
communication concern select only the needed fields:
`job | sender/authority | audience/context/prior knowledge |
interpretation/action | supported proposition/evidence | modal relation |
misreading/consequence | protected content | field owner/status/source version |
proof target | unknowns`. This record owns nothing: observers add attributed
observations; the field owner alone changes a decision/status. Source-version
changes invalidate affected fields rather than silently updating approval.

Use only fields that can change the work. Record each as
`value | status | evidence | owner | decision authority`, where status is
`supplied`, `observed`, `verified`, `inferred`, `assumed`, `contested`, or
`unknown`.

- **Outcome:** desired change, observable completion, consequence of failure.
- **People and context:** actual actor or audience, task, prior knowledge,
  motivation/barrier, environment, distance/device/channel, access needs. Never
  invent a persona to fill a gap.
- **Communication:** barrier, required message or action, message hierarchy,
  sender/authority, audience context and prior knowledge, supported proposition,
  intended interpretation/action or retention, modality relation, likely
  misreading and consequence, proof target, tone boundary, and the requested
  deliverable kept as a solution request rather than silently restated as the
  problem.
- **Content manifest:** exact required strings, claims, data, assets, warnings,
  instructions, legal/access content, credits, owner, source state, allowed
  transformation, and missing material.
- **Delivery context:** artifact set, medium, dimensions, duration, locale,
  incumbent brand/product system and its source/version/currentness, production
  and maintenance conditions.
- **Decision space:** classify each item as `invariant`, `requirement`,
  `evidence-bounded constraint`, `preference`, `open choice`, or `unknown`; name
  who may change it.
- **Risk and authority:** prohibited claims/assets, rights/culture/privacy risks,
  budget/time or provider assumptions, reviewers, approval owner, and unresolved
  dependencies outside Design.

Corporate Identity strategy, organisational behaviour/culture, naming and
verbal identity are upstream inputs. Do not convert them into Design-owned facts
or route them as visual-system work unless an actual corporate-design or visual-
identity decision is open.

Write the style-free frame as:
`For [actor/context], enable [outcome/action] by communicating [content] past
[barrier], while preserving [hard constraints]; [deliverable] is the requested
vehicle, not the problem definition.`

## Decide criteria before concepts

Separate **hard gates** from **comparative criteria**. A gate has an authority,
evidence state, observable failure, and disposition if unmet. Comparative
criteria describe trade-offs such as communication precision, subject
specificity, task fit, format survival, access, feasibility, maintainability,
production risk, and distinctiveness; define what evidence would change the
judgment. Do not add criteria after seeing a preferred direction or collapse
unknowns into a score. Distinguish business/content approval, professional
design judgment, access/production authority, and intended-audience evidence.

The output is a compact frame, content manifest, decision-space map, gate and
criterion record, approval map, and consequential unknown list. It does not
choose fonts, palette, style, layout, or concept unless one is already an
authorised constraint.

## Translate a vague request into a decision

Fictional example: “Make the lending desk look lively.” Supplied task: visitors
must identify an available object and its collection time. A useful frame is
`availability leads; object identity supports comparison; collection time stays
attached; eligibility rules are unknown`. “Lively” can remain a preference,
but it does not answer what should lead. Check whether the intended reader can
find the object/time relation in the proposed artifact; do not fill the unknown
eligibility rule with plausible policy. A settled request to correct one date
needs neither this example's framing work nor a new visual direction.

## Critique and smallest repair

Look for these failure signatures and test the parent cause:

- **Deliverable-as-problem:** the frame says “make a poster/site.” Recover the
  outcome, audience task, message and barrier; preserve the requested vehicle as
  a constraint or hypothesis.
- **Fluent fiction:** audience, strategy, history, feasibility, rights, or
  success is plausible but unsourced. Relabel it `inferred/assumed/unknown` and
  name the owner or evidence needed; preserve verified facts.
- **Hidden content:** real long copy, warning, datum, credit, state, or asset is
  absent. Restore it to the manifest and revise affected hierarchy or gate;
  preserve exact supplied wording and provenance.
- **False lock or false freedom:** a preference is treated as law, or an
  invariant as optional. Correct classification and authority only; preserve
  unaffected decisions.
- **Retrofitted criteria:** the favourite explains the rubric. Return to the
  outcome and evidence, freeze gates and criteria, and record the revision.
- **Approval confusion:** stakeholder taste is reported as user evidence or a
  technical check as design approval. Separate authority lanes and unknowns.

Record every frame change as `field | old state | new state | reason/evidence |
authority | affected decisions`. Re-evaluate only affected gates, content and
downstream decisions; do not silently move the goalposts.

## Exceptions

A tiny reversible task, locked production edit, emergency, or mature system may
need only the missing decisive fields. A quiet utility artifact may use
familiarity, progressive disclosure, error prevention, direct comparison, or
one-decision-at-a-time as its organising rationale; novelty and a memorable
signature are not universal gates. A single direction is acceptable when the
path is authorised and uncertainty is low, but record why exploration adds no
decision value. New verified evidence or the authorised owner may revise a
constraint; model confidence may not.

## Proof

Check that every required field resolves to inspected evidence or an explicit
unknown; every content item, gate, criterion and approval has an owner; no
assumption is phrased as fact; and the requested deliverable is distinguishable
from the desired outcome. A rough may expose a false frame: append a revision,
invalidate affected judgments, and reassess rather than rewriting history.
Completion of this record proves only that the design problem is explicit and
traceable, not that research occurred or that the proposed outcome will succeed.

## Ownership and claim ceiling

Design converts supplied and verified knowledge into a usable decision frame.
Strategy, research, facts, policy, rights, culture, production and final
approval remain with their actual owners. Do not claim user-centred,
validated, feasible, accessible, production-ready, legally cleared, or
strategically correct without the evidence and authority specific to that
claim.
